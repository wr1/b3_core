Concepts

Resin halo

Graded stiffness from opened foam cells along saw-cuts — explained with graphics.

Grid-scoring does more than cut a neat kerf: the saw opens foam cells along the walls and root. During infusion those cells take resin, so the real section is graded — not a binary foam | resin paint job.

What the halo looks like

Through-thickness view of one kerf: neat resin in the slot, a resin-rich halo of opened cells, then intact foam. Halo width tracks foam cell size (reach ≈ cell_size).

Laustsen et al. (2014) CT scans show this band is wider than the nominal slit and depends on density — smaller cells (e.g. H130) → thinner halo than larger cells (e.g. H60). b3_core models that band explicitly instead of folding it into a single calibrated failure strain.

How stiffness is graded

Distance d [mm] is measured from each foam Gauss point to the nearest cut surface (groove walls + root). Unsawn outer faces use a thinner closed-cell halo (default 0.25 × saw-cut reach).

S(d) is the cell-size survival: S(0)=1 at the cut, S→0 at reach.

Bigger cells ⇒ longer reach ⇒ wider resin-rich band ⇒ higher homogenized moduli (tests: Ezz rises with cell_size).

Backend and outputs

Halo needs per-Gauss-point stiffness → cases with core.cell_size auto-route to numpy. Results include:

FieldMeaning
resin_vfNeat kerf resin volume fraction
halo_vfExtra resin from opened cells
effective_resin_vfresin_vf + halo_vf
rho_infusedDensity including halo resin

Engineering constants already embed the grade — no separate halo layer in the global FEA model.

When mould curvature is set, the halo grades off the tapered wall hw(z) — see Halo + curvature (compose figures + Eyy vs κ × cell_size).
